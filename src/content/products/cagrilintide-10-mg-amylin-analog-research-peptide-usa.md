## Research Background

Cagrilintide is a long-acting amylin analogue engineered for metabolic research. Amylin is a 37-amino acid peptide hormone co-secreted with insulin from pancreatic β-cells that regulates postprandial glucose through three complementary mechanisms: delayed gastric emptying, suppressed glucagon secretion, and central appetite modulation via the area postrema. Cagrilintide's design incorporates strategic amino acid substitutions that confer amylin receptor (AMY-R) selectivity and an extended pharmacokinetic profile suitable for chronic metabolic research protocols. As the amylin pathway is mechanistically independent from the GLP-1 incretin pathway, Cagrilintide is frequently studied in combination with GLP-1 receptor agonists (Semaglutide, Tirzepatide) to evaluate dual-pathway metabolic intervention strategies.

## Molecular Mechanisms

### Amylin Receptor (AMY-R) Agonism

Selective activation of AMY-R (CTR/RAMP1 complex) in the area postrema → central appetite suppression and delayed gastric emptying. AMY-R signaling is independent of GLP-1R and GIPR — targeting a distinct node in metabolic regulation.

### Postprandial Glucagon Suppression

Amylin-mediated suppression of pancreatic α-cell glucagon secretion → reduced hepatic glucose output after meals. This complements GLP-1's insulinotropic effect, providing dual control over postprandial glucose.

### Gastric Emptying Delay

AMY-R activation on vagal afferents → reduced gastric motility → slower nutrient delivery to the small intestine → attenuated postprandial glucose excursions. Synergistic with GLP-1's gastric emptying effects.

### Cagrilintide + Semaglutide Synergy

Both slow gastric emptying, but through different receptor systems. The Cag/Sema combination targets both pathways.
