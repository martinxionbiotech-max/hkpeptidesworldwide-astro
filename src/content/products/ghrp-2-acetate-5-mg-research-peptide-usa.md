## Research Background

GHRP-2 (Pralmorelin) is a synthetic hexapeptide ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a) agonist developed by Kaken Pharmaceutical. It is one of the most potent GH secretagogues in the GHRP family, producing rapid, robust GH pulses through GHS-R1a activation on pituitary somatotrophs. Strategic D-amino acid modifications (D-Ala¹, D-2-Nal², D-Phe⁵) confer both high receptor affinity and resistance to enzymatic degradation. Compared to Ipamorelin (cleaner, more selective) and GHRP-6 (stronger hunger signaling), GHRP-2 occupies the middle ground — potent GH release with moderate selectivity. The acetate salt form provides optimal solubility for laboratory reconstitution.

## Molecular Mechanisms

### GHS-R1a Receptor Agonism

High-affinity ghrelin receptor binding → Gαq/PLC/IP₃ signaling → intracellular Ca²⁺ mobilization → GH vesicle exocytosis from pituitary somatotrophs. GHRP-2 demonstrates sub-nanomolar GHS-R1a affinity in competitive binding assays.

### GHRH Signal Potentiation

GHS-R1a and GHRH-R pathways converge on the somatotroph via distinct second messengers (PLC/Ca²⁺ vs cAMP/PKA). Simultaneous activation produces synergistic GH release exceeding additive effects — the basis for CJC-1295 + GHRP-2 combination protocols.

### Somatostatin Suppression

GHS-R1a activation in hypothalamic arcuate nucleus → reduced somatostatin release → removal of tonic GH inhibition → permissive window for amplified GH secretion.

**Q: How does GHRP-2 differ from Ipamorelin and GHRP-6?**
